About Bao

Bao la Kiswahili, commonly called Bao, is a traditional East African mancala strategy board game especially associated with Zanzibar and mainland Tanzania. Players sow kete (seeds or pieces) through rows of pits, capture opposing pieces, and try to empty the opponent's front row or leave the opponent without a legal move.

This free browser version lets you play Bao online against the computer AI or as a local two-player board game on the same device. Once the game files are cached, it also works offline.

How to Play / Rules Used

In computer mode, choose whether to play first (SOUTH) or second (NORTH). In local two-player mode, SOUTH and NORTH take turns on the same device. Choose a highlighted pit, then choose a sowing direction.

During namua, one kete is added from hand on each move. When both players' hands are empty, the game enters mtaji. You win by emptying the opponent's front row or leaving the opponent with no legal move.

Terms: kete are the playing pieces, KICHWA are the entry pits at the two ends, and NYUMBA is the house near the center. On a CAPTURE, the opponent's kete in the facing front-row pit are taken and sown from either the left or right KICHWA side.

Choices: LEFT/RIGHT are the sowing directions from the selected pit. LEFT CAPTURE/RIGHT CAPTURE mean entering captured kete from the left/right KICHWA side. NYUMBA STOP stops at the house; NYUMBA USE uses the house kete and continues relay sowing.

This game is a prototype. Rules, presentation, and behavior may change in future versions.

Bao la Kiswahili Complete Guide (Japanese) uses public draft v0.1.0-draft, rule baseline R-002. Unverified takasia is not applied. A safety limit is used for relay sowing that does not terminate.
